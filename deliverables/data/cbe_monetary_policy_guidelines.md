### PREAMBLE AND MACROECONOMIC CONTEXT

In accordance with the Central Bank of Egypt and Banking Sector Law No. 194 of 2020, the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) is mandated to ensure price stability and safeguard the soundness of the Egyptian banking and financial system. Against a backdrop of persistent global inflationary pressures, prolonged supply chain disruptions, and shifting international monetary conditions, the CBE remains committed to proactive macroeconomic management. 

This comprehensive circular outlines the updated monetary policy stance, inflation targeting framework, foreign exchange (FX) management protocols for the Egyptian Pound (EGP), and strict regulatory compliance mandates for all investment banks, brokerage firms, and asset managers operating within the Arab Republic of Egypt, including those trading on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX).

All licensed financial institutions, including tier-one investment management firms, are required to immediately integrate these directives into their risk management, portfolio rebalancing, and client advisory frameworks.

---

### SECTION 1: INTEREST RATE DECISIONS AND MONETARY POLICY STANCE

#### 1.1 Current Corridor Rates
Following the latest assembly of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), the CBE has evaluated current domestic liquidity constraints and core inflation trajectories. To anchor inflation expectations and mitigate second-round effects of recent supply shocks, the MPC has decreed an adjustment to the core policy rates.

Effective immediately, the CBE's policy rates are established as follows:
*   **Overnight Deposit Rate:** 19.25%
*   **Overnight Lending Rate:** 20.25%
*   **Rate of the Main Operation:** 19.75%
*   **Discount Rate:** 19.75%

#### 1.2 Rationale and Transmission Mechanism
The restrictive monetary policy stance is designed to absorb excess systemic liquidity. Historical data indicates that the transmission mechanism of these policy rates to the broader Egyptian economy takes approximately three to four quarters. 

Investment firms managing EGX-listed equities and domestic fixed-income portfolios must account for higher cost-of-capital assumptions in their discounted cash flow (DCF) models. Furthermore, yields on Egyptian Treasury Bills (T-Bills) and sovereign bonds are expected to re-price upward in the primary market auctions, providing lucrative yield opportunities for fixed-income allocations, while simultaneously pressuring highly leveraged corporate entities listed on the EGX.

#### 1.3 Forward Guidance
The MPC emphasizes that the current trajectory of policy rates will remain strictly data-dependent. Future interest rate normalizations will only be considered once urban headline inflation consistently tracks within the CBE’s designated target band for three consecutive quarters.

### SECTION 2: INFLATION TARGETING FRAMEWORK

#### 2.1 Baseline Target and Tolerance Bands
Price stability remains the paramount objective of the CBE. The MPC has reaffirmed its inflation targeting framework to guide macroeconomic expectations. 
*   **Primary Target:** 7.0% 
*   **Tolerance Band:** ± 2.0 percentage points (i.e., 5.0% to 9.0%)
*   **Target Horizon:** Q4 of the upcoming fiscal year.

#### 2.2 Core vs. Headline Inflation Dynamics
Financial analysts must differentiate between urban headline inflation, published by the Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS), and core inflation, computed by the CBE. Core inflation structurally excludes highly volatile food items and regulated prices to provide a clearer view of underlying demand-pull pressures. 

Institutional portfolios heavily weighted in the consumer staples, building materials, and real estate sectors must be stress-tested against potential contractions in consumer purchasing power if core inflation breaches the 15% threshold for more than two consecutive quarters.

### SECTION 3: FOREIGN EXCHANGE (FX) AND EGP DIRECTIVES

#### 3.1 Exchange Rate Regime
The Central Bank of Egypt reiterates its absolute commitment to a durably flexible exchange rate regime. The valuation of the Egyptian Pound (EGP) against the US Dollar (USD) and a broader basket of international currencies shall be determined strictly by market forces of supply and demand. This flexibility is fundamental to absorbing external shocks and maintaining the competitiveness of Egyptian exports.

#### 3.2 FX Liquidity Management and Interbank Protocols
To ensure the orderly functioning of the FX market, licensed banks and investment firms must adhere to the following strict liquidity protocols:
1.  **Prioritization of Essential Imports:** FX liquidity allocations must prioritize strategic commodities, pharmaceuticals, and essential industrial inputs. 
2.  **Repatriation of Capital:** Foreign investors operating on the EGX are guaranteed the right to repatriate dividends and principal capital; however, clearing processes must be scheduled through the official interbank market to prevent severe intraday EGP volatility.
3.  **Prohibition of Speculative Arbitrage:** Financial institutions are strictly prohibited from engaging in unhedged, speculative short-selling of the EGP. Proprietary trading desks caught utilizing offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) to artificially manipulate onshore exchange rates will face immediate suspension of their operating licenses.

#### 3.3 Guidance for Asset Managers (EGP Devaluation Scenarios)
In periods of rapid EGP exchange rate adjustments, asset managers are advised to recalibrate client portfolios to hedge against currency depreciation. Acceptable hedging strategies include:
*   Increasing weightings in EGX-listed companies with substantial export revenues (e.g., fertilizers, petrochemicals).
*   Allocating to dual-listed equities with global depository receipts (GDRs).
*   Investing in domestic gold-backed investment funds approved by the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA).

### SECTION 4: REGULATORY COMPLIANCE FOR INVESTMENT AND BROKERAGE FIRMS

The following compliance mandates are legally binding for all investment management firms, advisory services, and brokerage houses regulated by the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) and the CBE.

#### 4.1 Fiduciary Duty and Information Dissemination
Financial analysts and portfolio managers carry a fiduciary duty to provide clients with accurate, verified, and contextualized macroeconomic analyses. The dissemination of unsubstantiated rumors regarding CBE policy, EGP devaluation targets, or emergency MPC meetings is a severe criminal offense under the Egyptian Penal Code (Market Manipulation clauses).

#### 4.2 AI and Automated Advisory Systems (Strict Adherence Required)
In light of the rapid adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Large Language Models (LLMs), and automated reporting systems by prominent Cairo-based investment firms, the CBE and FRA have instituted emergency prudential regulations:

1.  **Prohibition of Fully Autonomous Publishing:** No automated AI system, algorithmic analyst, or LLM may directly publish, broadcast, or email macroeconomic policy interpretations or investment advice to clients without human intervention.
2.  **Mandatory Evaluator-Optimizer Architecture:** Firms employing AI for data synthesis must utilize a multi-layered validation architecture. Generated reports must be computationally evaluated against real-time market data and official CBE circulars to prevent "hallucinations" or factual inaccuracies.
3.  **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gateway:** All AI-generated financial briefs, particularly those discussing EGP exchange rates or monetary policy, must enter a mandatory "Pre-publish hold state." A designated, licensed human compliance officer, Director of Research, or Chief Investment Officer (CIO) must physically review, authorize, and manually trigger the release of the document. 
4.  **Audit Trails:** Firms must maintain immutable, cryptographic logs (or equivalent traceable database records) of all AI prompts, source document retrievals (e.g., Vector Database/RAG logs), intermediate agent reasoning steps, and the final timestamp of the human director's approval. These logs must be retained for a minimum of five (5) years and made available to CBE auditors upon 24 hours' notice.

#### 4.3 Capital Adequacy under Stress
Investment firms must maintain a minimum Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) of 12.5%. Risk management departments must submit quarterly stress tests detailing the impact of a hypothetical 20% parallel shift in the EGP/USD exchange rate and a 300-basis-point upward shock to the yield curve on their tier-1 capital reserves.

---

### SECTION 5: LIQUIDITY MANAGEMENT & OPEN MARKET OPERATIONS (OMO)

To maintain precise control over systemic liquidity, the CBE utilizes Open Market Operations as its primary liquidity management tool. 

#### 5.1 Reserve Requirement Ratio (RRR)
The mandatory Reserve Requirement Ratio (RRR) for local commercial banks remains fixed at 18.0%. This non-remunerated reserve requirement acts as a structural liquidity buffer. Investment firms partnered with local retail banks for client deposit sweeping must ensure their partners are not utilizing high-yield retail products to circumvent RRR obligations.

#### 5.2 Deposit Auctions
The CBE conducts fixed-rate and variable-rate deposit auctions on a weekly basis to absorb structural excess liquidity. Financial institutions must closely monitor the accepted cutoff yields at these auctions, as they serve as the most accurate leading indicator of short-term Egyptian pound interbank rates (CAIBOR).

---

### SECTION 6: ENFORCEMENT, PENALTIES, AND AUDIT RIGHTS

The Central Bank of Egypt, in coordination with the FRA, maintains a zero-tolerance policy for non-compliance with monetary policy directives or risk-management negligence.

#### 6.1 Regulatory Audits
The CBE's Financial Regulatory Compliance Sector reserves the right to conduct unannounced, on-site inspections of investment firms. Auditors will specifically target the operational integrity of algorithmic trading desks, AI-driven advisory systems, and FX exposure management.

#### 6.2 Penalties for Non-Compliance
Violations of the protocols outlined in this circular, particularly those relating to FX manipulation, circumvention of Human-in-the-Loop AI publishing rules, or the dissemination of unverified macroeconomic data, will result in the following escalating penalties:
1.  **Level 1:** Official censure and mandatory suspension of the offending department head.
2.  **Level 2:** Punitive fines up to EGP 50,000,000.
3.  **Level 3:** Permanent revocation of the firm’s operating license and criminal referral to the Economic Court of Cairo.

#### 6.3 Implementation
The Board of Directors of all addressed institutions must formally acknowledge receipt of this circular. Internal compliance policies must be updated and submitted to the CBE’s Regulatory portal no later than 14 business days from the date of issuance.
